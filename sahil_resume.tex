\section{Key Project | Research-Oriented}

\subsection{\href{https://github.com/Sahil-M-Deo/Randomised-Algorithms}{\textcolor{Green}{Randomised Algorithms: A 137-Page Monograph}}}{Summer of Science}{2026}{\textit{Maths and Physics Club, IIT Bombay} \hfill \href{https://github.com/Sahil-M-Deo/Randomised-Algorithms}{\small\faGithub\ Sahil-M-Deo/Randomised-Algorithms}}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Authored a self-contained \textbf{137-page, 13-chapter book} in \LaTeX{} as an extension of the project report, with \textbf{6 appendices} on probability, concentration inequalities, limit theorems, finite fields and linear algebra, and \textbf{original end-of-chapter problem sets}
    \item Covered \textbf{RP, ZPP \& BPP}, analysis of QuickSort and QuickSelect, \textbf{universal \& $k$-wise independent hashing}, \textbf{Freivalds' algorithm}, skip lists, treaps, Bloom filters, cuckoo hashing, \textbf{Miller--Rabin}, \textbf{Pollard's rho}, streaming (\textbf{Flajolet--Martin, HyperLogLog}), zero-knowledge proofs and \textbf{derandomisation}
    \item Using the \textbf{probabilistic method}, designed Las Vegas algorithms for dominating set and max-cut; proved a success probability of at least $\frac{1}{m+1}$ for finding a cut with $\ge m/2$ edges, giving $\Theta(nm)$ expected time
    \item \textbf{Conjectured} from large-scale simulation (exhaustive enumeration for small $n$, Monte Carlo sampling for large $n$) that the \textbf{5-cycle is the worst-case graph} for the randomised \textbf{max-cut} algorithm, with success probability $\frac{5}{16}$; this would tighten the bound to $\Theta(n+m)$ expected time
    \item Proved via a \textbf{rank argument} on $\pm1$ orthogonal columns that generating $n$ pairwise-independent bits needs $\ge \log_2(n+1)$ truly random bits, tight for the \textbf{XOR construction}; derandomised max-cut via pairwise independence and the \textbf{method of conditional expectations}
    \item Used \textbf{Monte Carlo sampling} (10,000 trials per $n$) to validate an \textbf{approximate-independence} assumption in a probabilistic construction, and to show the \textbf{union bound} is near-tight in practice
\end{itemize}
